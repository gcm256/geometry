## Emergent / Bulk / Statistical / Macroscopic Properties[^1]

Eg Temperature, Pressure etc.

| Macroscopic Property (Bulk Matter) | Microscopic / Quantum Counterpart | Physical Description of the Transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Temperature** | Average translational kinetic energy of particles | Individual atoms have velocity, not temperature. The bulk average of this kinetic motion manifests macroscopically as heat. |
| **Pressure** | Time-averaged momentum transfer from particle collisions | Trillions of isolated molecules colliding with a container wall exert tiny individual forces that smooth out into a steady macroscopic pressure. |
| **Entropy** | Number of available microscopic configurations (Microstates) | Macroscopic disorder is mathematically defined by Boltzmann's equation ($S = k_B \ln \Omega$), counting how many ways particles can arrange themselves. |
| **Internal Energy ($U$)** | Sum of all microscopic kinetic and potential energies | The total energy stored within the system's molecular bonds, atomic vibrations, and particle velocities. |
| **Volume** | Spatial boundaries dictated by electron cloud repulsion | Matter feels solid and takes up bulk space because the electron clouds of individual atoms strongly repel each other (Pauli Exclusion Principle). |
| **Viscosity** | Intermolecular friction and momentum exchange | A fluid's resistance to flow is caused by neighboring molecules pulling on each other via intermolecular forces as they slide past. |
| **Surface Tension** | Net cohesive forces acting on boundary molecules | Molecules at a liquid's surface lack neighbors above them, creating an imbalance of microscopic attractive forces that pulls the surface taut. |
| **State of Matter (Phase)** | Collective balance between kinetic energy and binding forces | Phases emerge when the average kinetic energy of particles either succumbs to (solid) or overcomes (gas) the attractive intermolecular potentials. |
| **Electrical Conductivity** | Mobility and density of free charge carriers | Bulk current flow is the macroscopic result of trillions of free electrons (in metals) or ions (in electrolytes) drifting through a lattice. |
| **Thermal Conductivity** | Transport of phonons and free electrons | Heat moves through bulk material via the microscopic passing of atomic vibrations (phonons) or mobile electrons from particle to particle. |
| **Density** | Average atomic mass packed within a lattice volume | While a single atom has a fixed mass, bulk density depends on how closely the atomic structures pack together under macroscopic forces. |
| **Refractive Index** | Microscopic scattering and absorption by electron clouds | Light slows down in bulk matter because photons are continuously absorbed and re-emitted by the electron clouds of the material's atoms. |

## Randomness vs Disorder / Chaos / Entropy / Information-content

**Randomness is uncertainty, unpredictability of an event.**

An event is random iff its probability is strictly between 0 and 1 (excluding 0 and 1). 
So, for event $E$, $E$ is random iff $0 < P(E) < 1$.

> [!NOTE]
> "Equal-likelihood" is a special kind of randomness, when a random variable $X$ takes all its possible values with equal probabilities, ie all values of $X$ are equally likely.
>

Randomness is deeply tied to the observer's knowledge, and to the scale of observation. Eg molecules of a gas seem to move randomly at molecular scale, but:
1. When we zoom out to macroscopic scale, we can see predictable (non-random) patterns of Temperature, Pressure etc.
2. When we zoom in to quantum-mechanical or sub-atomic scale, we can know the exact "forces" and interactions, and can predict the movement of all the molecules using the rules of QM and mechanics of collisions. So at this scale the movement of gas molecules is not random either.

**Disorder/Entropy is the proportion of information that cannot be left out (omitted) during communicating it loss-lessly.** 

So, 100% disordered (completely chaotic) information can only be loss-lessly transmitted or saved without any compression or omission.

Therefore, meaning is the omitted part. **Meaning of a message, is the information that can be discarded / omitted from the message by the sender before transmission, and still the receiver can losslessly reconstruct entire intended message, including the omitted part**, ie the receiver can know its meaning, even if the sender did not send all of it.[^2]

Therefore, more disorder corresponds to less meaning, and vice versa.

Complete Order = 0% Disorder
Complete Chaos = 100% Disorder

Complexity lies between Complete Order and Complete Disorder.[^3]
